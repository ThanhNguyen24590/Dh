#### 1: FOCUS ON THE PRESENT
* Take in everything presented to the senses. Allow attention to tune in to and range freely among any of the sounds, bodily sensations, smells, or thoughts.
* Allow attention go to any sensations that attract it. Don’t analyze or think about them. Thoughts (beyond just noticing and recognizing) take you away from the present.
* Observe sensations objectively. Be skeptical with thoughts.
* If find a particular sensation to be pleasant, enjoy it. Let that condition your mind toward a state of happiness. Savoring the subjective quality of pleasure, not the sense object.
* If find a particular sensation to be unpleasant, distinguish between: reaction and the object that produced it, then let go of the reaction.
* Dealing with distractions: let it come, let it be, let it go.

#### 2: FOCUS ON BODILY SENSATIONS
Limit attention to bodily sensations. Physical sensations: touch, pressure, warmth, coolness, movement, tingling,....Deep visceral sensations: rumbling in your stomach,...
* Nothing be suppressed\excluded from field of conscious awareness. Attention: limited to bodily sensations. Peripheral awareness: everything else.
* Release any tension you find and make final adjustments to your posture.
* Notice any pleasant sensations, distinguishing between the sensation as sensation and your mind’s reaction to it, enjoy and explore them freely.
	* Feelings of air moving over the skin, warmth or coolness, and the softness or supportive firmness of the meditation cushion.
	* Pleasant sensations deeper in your muscles and joints as you relax, or warm feelings in your chest and abdomen.
	* Overall pleasant sense of stillness, peace, contentment.
* When you start feeling restless or your sense of contentment fades, return to step one.

#### 3: FOCUS ON BODILY SENSATIONS RELATED TO THE BREATH.
* Naturally gravitate toward the sensations of movement produced by breathing.
* Take time to familiar with all breath-related sensations. In particular, savor any pleasant qualities associated with them.
* Notice the mind becomes mildly invigorated during the in-breath, while the out-breath feels more relaxing and soothing.
* Attention: breath-related sensations + Peripheral Awareness: Without suppressing anything else.
* Once you settle in, start focusing more directly on the sensations of the breath in specific areas.
	* the rise and fall of the abdomen
	* the expansion and contraction of the chest
	* the sensations produced by air moving in and out of the nostrils
* Allow your mind to move freely among the abdomen, chest, nose, and anywhere else where you feel breath-related sensations.
* Breathe naturally. Be a passive observer, noticing any sensations that happen to be present.
* Take note of, savor, and even purposely induce feelings of peace and happiness, especially as your attention becomes more stable and you experience more inner calm.

##### 4: FOCUS ON SENSATIONS OF THE BREATH AT THE NOSE.
Direct your attention to the sensations produced by the air moving in and out of your nostrils.
Locate where those sensations are clearest:
* just inside the nostrils
* at the tip of the nose
* on the upper lip, or wherever else. 
location of sensations may not be quite the same for the in- and out-breaths.

Keep your attention on the area where the breath sensations are clearest. Don’t try to follow the air.Just observe the sensations from the air passing over the spot where you’re focusing your attention. Remember, the meditation object is the sensations of the breath, not the breath itself.

If your attention wanders, gently bring it back.
