##### Heraññakani https://suttacentral.net/thag2.13/en/sujato
Accayanti ahorattā, jīvitaṃ uparujjhati; Āyu khīyati maccānaṃ, kunnadīnaṃva odakaṃ.
Atha pāpāni kammāni, karaṃ bālo na bujjhati; Pacchāssa kaṭukaṃ hoti, vipāko hissa pāpako.

 Days & nights fly past. Life end. 
 Spans of mortals wasted, away like ticking stream's water.
 The fool does not see: unskillful deeds bring bitter fruits.

###### Thag 6.13 Sirimanda
Make the day not-in-vain, a little or a lot. However much the day passes, that's how much less is your life. Your last day approaches. This isn't your time to be heedless.

###### Valliya (Thag 2.24)
What needs to be done with firm persistence, what needs to be done by someone who hopes for Awakening, that I will do. I will not fail. See: persistence & striving! You show me the path: straight, coming ashore in the Deathless. I, through sagacity, will reach it, know it, as the stream of the Ganges, the sea.

###### Matangaputta Thag 3.5
It's too cold, too hot, too late in the evening — people who say this, shirking their work: the moment passes them by. Whoever regards cold & heat as no more than grass, doing his manly duties, won't fall away from ease. With my chest I push through wild grasses — spear-grass, ribbon-grass, rushes — cultivating a seclusion heart.

###### Dhp V Balavagga: The Fool 
Long is the night to the sleepless; long is the league to the weary. Long is worldly existence to fools who know not the Sublime Truth.

###### Dhp II Appamadavagga
Heedfulness: the path to the Deathless. Heedlessness: the path to death. The heedful do not die. The heedless are as if already dead. Knowing this as a true distinction, those wise in heedfulness rejoice in heedfulness, enjoying the range of the noble ones.

###### Dhp VIII Sahassavagga: The Thousands 
Better to live one day virtuous and meditative than a hundred years immoral and uncontrolled.
Better to live one day wise and meditative than a hundred years confused and uncentered, bewilded.
Better to live one day strenuous and resolute than to live a hundred years apathetic and unenergetic.
Though one may conquer thousands men in battle, yet he indeed is the greater victor who conquers himself.

##### Bhuta Thera Thag 9
When the thundering storm cloud roars out in the mist, And torrents of rain fill the paths of the birds, Nestled in a mountain cave, the monk meditates. — No greater contentment than this can be found.
When along the rivers the tumbling flowers bloom In winding wreaths adorned with verdant color, Seated on the bank, glad-minded, he meditates. — No greater contentment than this can be found. 
When in the depths of night, in a lonely forest, The rain-deva drizzles and the fanged beasts cry, Nestled in a mountain cave, the monk meditates. — No greater contentment than this can be found. When restraining himself and his discursive thoughts, (Dwelling in a hollow in the mountains' midst), Devoid of fear and barrenness, he meditates. — No greater contentment than this can be found.
When he is happy — expunged of stain, waste and grief, Unobstructed, unencumbered, unassailed — Having ended all defilements, he meditates. — No greater contentment than this can be found.

##### Sambādhasutta https://suttacentral.net/an9.42/en/sujato
Sambādhe gataṁ okāsaṁ
Avidvā bhūrimedhaso,
Yo jhānamabujjhi buddho,
Paṭilīnanisabho munī.

	From prison of sensual pleasue confined,
	The great Sage opened the Path,
	Through the steps of Jhana,
	The lone Arahat, teacher of whom wish to learn.
